# Chives

- Botanical name: Allium schoenoprasum [NC State Extension]
- Family: Alliaceae [hand-entered, following the green onion entry]

## Sowing
- Sowing depth mm: 6 [Utah State Extension, Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Light needed to germinate: not stated by any source reached
- Row spacing cm: 30 [UC Master Gardeners of Orange County]
- Plant spacing cm: 10 to 15 [Utah State Extension]
- Plant spacing disagreement: figures run from 5 to 50 cm and do not resolve, so the Utah State figure is used as the working one [Johnny's 2 to 8 in, UC EHN 70 6 in, UC Santa Clara 8 to 12 in, Wisconsin and UC Sacramento 12 to 20 in]
- Germination window days: 7 to 14 at 65 to 70 F [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Germination by a slower account: 4 to 6 weeks to reach transplant size [Illinois Extension]
- Local sowing months: January and February optimal, March and October to December acceptable, September not marked at all [UC Master Gardeners of Orange County Seed Planting Chart]
- Seed strain matters: generic seed gives variable leaf texture and size, so buy a named culinary type [Illinois Extension]

## Growing
- Days to harvest from seed: 60, or 30 from a transplant [Utah State Extension]
- Harvest can start at height: 6 in [NC State Extension]
- Mature size: 12 to 20 in tall and wide [UC Master Gardeners of Sacramento County]
- Sun: full sun to part shade, where the partial shade band is 2 to 6 h of direct sun a day [NC State Extension]
- Morning-only sun: listed among the partial-shade tolerant herbs [UC Master Gardeners of San Luis Obispo County]
- Light shade effect: reduces flowering, which is a gain rather than a loss here because flowering is what interrupts leaf production [University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension]
- Target soil pH: not stated by any source reached
- Water: needs regular watering with soil held near field capacity, which is what makes it compatible with a vegetable bed where the Mediterranean herbs are not [Utah State Extension]
- Water by a drier account: drought tolerant once established, though still best with consistent moisture [University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension]
- Root rot risk: occurs in poorly drained soil [NC State Extension]
- Crop coefficient: 1.0 [hand-entered, small-vegetable class after FAO-56 Table 12, cited not copied]

## Raised beds and containers
- Root depth: shallow, so cultivate carefully nearby [University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension]
- Minimum soil depth inches: 6 [UC Tulare and Kings Master Gardener Program]
- Raised bed: fits easily [hand-entered]

## Soil and rotation
- Do not follow: Alliaceae [hand-entered, following the green onion entry]

## Bolting and flowering
- Flowering rather than bolting is the thing to prevent: deadhead before the flowers fade or it self-seeds across the bed [University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension, Illinois Extension, UC Master Gardeners of Sacramento County]
- Invasive listing: absent from the Cal-IPC inventory despite those self-seeding warnings [Cal-IPC Inventory]
- Chilling requirement: none stated by any source reached
- Winter foliage: evergreen in mild climates rather than dying back, though one UC county source says it goes dormant in fall [University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension and NC State Extension for evergreen, UC Master Gardeners of Napa County for dormancy]

## Harvest
- Repeat harvest: yes, cut back to within 1 to 2 in of the soil, 3 to 4 times in the first year and monthly after that [Utah State Extension]
- Cut part of a clump and not all of it: shearing a whole clump costs several weeks of regrowth [University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension]
- Cut back after flowering to force new tender leaves: to about 3 in high [University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension]
- Division interval years: 2 to 5, with sources spread across that range [Utah State and Wisconsin 2 to 3, UC Santa Clara 2 to 4, Johnny's 3 to 4, UC EHN 70 3 to 5]
- Hardiness zone disagreement: UC Sacramento gives zones 3 to 10 and is the only range covering this site, against Johnny's 3 to 9 and NC State 4a to 8b, and none of the capping sources states a reason [UC Master Gardeners of Sacramento County, Johnny's, NC State Extension]
- Pest watch: black bean aphid, which resists a water spray, so cut to the ground and discard a heavy infestation [UC Master Gardeners of San Luis Obispo County]

# Shallot

- Botanical name: Allium cepa var. aggregatum [hand-entered]
- Family: Alliaceae [hand-entered, following the green onion entry]

## Sowing
- Planting time: bulbs go in during fall for a winter and spring harvest [Clemson HGIC]
- Planting time by a colder-climate account: spring as soon as the ground can be worked, or autumn after the first frost for a late spring harvest [Utah State Extension]
- Sets or seed: usually planted as bulb sets, though seed also works [Utah State Extension]
- Planting depth for sets inches: 1 to 2 [Clemson HGIC, Utah State Extension]
- Planting depth for seed inches: a quarter to a half [Utah State Extension]
- Set spacing inches: 6 to 8 in the row [Clemson HGIC]
- Set spacing by a tighter account: 3 to 6 in rows 12 to 18 in apart [Utah State Extension]
- Seed spacing: a half to three quarters of an inch, thinned to 2 to 4 in, in rows 1 to 2 ft apart [Utah State Extension]
- Density controls the outcome: close planting gives smaller single bulbs while wide spacing gives clusters, so spacing is the lever for which you want [Utah State Extension]
- Days to emergence: 10 to 14 [Utah State Extension]

## Growing
- Days to harvest for green use: 60 from planting [Clemson HGIC, Utah State Extension give 50 to 60]
- Days to harvest for dry bulbs: varies by variety, and the signal is the tops falling over rather than a date [Utah State Extension]
- Dry bulb timing in a mild climate: pulled in late spring once the tops have fully died down [Clemson HGIC]
- Curing weeks: 1 to 2 [Utah State Extension]
- Sun: full sun [Utah State Extension]
- Target soil pH: 5.5 to 7.5 [Utah State Extension]

## Whether shallots suit this garden at all, which is the first question
- Day-length sensitivity: all shallots have it, and should be planted as you would a long-day onion [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Adapted range: a band running from 36 to 55 degrees north, which begins well north of this garden at 33.6 [Territorial Seed]
- Best regions: intermediate and long day areas [Dixondale Farms]
- Per-cultivar range floors: the lowest found for any cultivar starts at 37 degrees north, and no cultivar reached is rated for a site this far south [Conservor at 37 to 60, with Ambition, Creme Brulee, Innovator and Matador all 40 to 60]
- Hardiness: zones 2 to 9, which excludes this site's 10b [Cornell]
- Local UC coverage: none, being absent from the statewide table entirely, with every UC source carrying shallots written for northern California [UC ANR Table 14.2 and county programs]
- Mild-winter failure mode: a fall planting through a long mild winter gives a long period where growth starts and stops, producing many growing points and smaller bulbs than a spring planting would [Oregon State Extension]
- The only UC sun figure for the crop: at least 8 hours of direct sun, roughly double this site's best cool-season month [UC Master Gardeners of Santa Clara County]
- Verdict: ruled out by day-length adaptation rather than by calendar, so the earlier idea of sourcing a short-day shallot does not work, because no short-day shallot was found to exist [hand-entered]
- Mother bulb size if tried anyway: use small ones, since bolting rose from 12.5 percent at 20 to 30 mm bulb diameter to 45.8 percent at 51 to 60 mm [Acta Agrobotanica 2013, on Ambition F1 and Matador F1]
- Do not bury the bulbs: shallot bulbs develop on top of the ground and should not be covered with soil [Clemson HGIC, Virginia Tech]
- Yield if tried: 5 to 10 bulbs per bulb planted, at about 90 to 120 days [UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County]

## Day length, which decides whether a variety works at all
- Bulbing is photoperiod controlled: onions and their relatives are classed long or short day by the hours of daylight needed to form a bulb [Clemson HGIC]
- Short day types: bulb at about 12 hours of daylight and suit gardens in the south [Clemson HGIC]
- Long day types: cannot be grown successfully in the South [Clemson HGIC]
- Consequence this far south: a long day variety may grow leaves perfectly and never form a usable bulb, so its day length class matters more than flavour or price [hand-entered, following Clemson]
- The workaround: harvesting green at about 60 days happens before bulbing, so day length stops mattering if the crop is used as a spring onion rather than grown for dry bulbs [hand-entered, following Clemson's green-use figure]

## Raised beds and containers
- Amend the top inches: 6 [Utah State Extension]
- Irrigate to a depth of inches: 8 to 12 [Utah State Extension]
- Raised bed: suits one, being a shallow-rooted allium, and an 8 in bed is adequate while 12 in is comfortable [hand-entered, following the irrigation and amendment depths above]

## Bolting
- A flowered plant is a lost crop: once an onion, shallot or leek flowers it will not form a marketable bulb or stalk [MSU Extension]
- Fall planting lowers the risk: fall-transplanted shallots are less likely to bolt than spring-sown alliums, because of a larger juvenile size requirement and longer vernalising time [MSU Extension]

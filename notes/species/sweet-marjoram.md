# Sweet Marjoram

- Botanical name: Origanum majorana [Illinois Extension]
- Family: Lamiaceae [hand-entered]

## Sowing
- Sowing depth mm: 0, surface sown and pressed in, because light is needed to germinate [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Light needed to germinate: yes, do not cover the seed [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Direct sowing rate: 2 to 3 seeds an inch in a narrow band, tamped into place [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Row spacing cm: 45 [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Plant spacing cm: 15 to 20 [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Plant spacing by UC accounts: 15 to 45 cm, wider than Johnny's [UC Tulare and Kings 6 to 12 in, UC EHN 70 12 in, UC Santa Clara 12 to 18 in, UC Sonoma 18 in]
- Germination window days: 7 to 14 at 65 to 70 F [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Germination by a slower account: seed is nearly dust-like and needs a long period to germinate, which disagrees with the figure above [UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County]
- Pretreatment: soaking the seed in water overnight is said to improve germination [Illinois Extension]
- Local sowing months: spring or fall, and it is absent from the UC Orange County Seed Planting Chart entirely [UC Tulare and Kings Master Gardener Program]

## Growing
- Days to harvest from seed: not stated as a day count by any source reached, and keyed to appearance instead
- Harvest indicator: ball-like tips appearing at the ends of the stems, which is where the common name knotted marjoram comes from [Illinois Extension]
- Mature size: 8 to 24 in tall [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Sun: full sun, meaning 6 or more hours of direct sun a day, and this is the consensus [NC State Extension, Johnny's Selected Seeds, Illinois Extension, UC EHN 70, Missouri Botanical Garden, Clemson HGIC]
- Morning sun allowance: one source permits morning sun with afternoon shade, and it is the outlier rather than the rule [UC Tulare and Kings Master Gardener Program]
- Partial shade otherwise: allowed only for variegated varieties [UC Santa Clara]
- Compare oregano: NC State gives Greek oregano the partial shade tag at 2 to 6 h and gives marjoram only the full sun tag, so marjoram is the worse of the two for a part-shade bed [NC State Extension]
- Target soil pH: not stated by any source reached
- Water: will not tolerate dry soil, and wants moderate water, which makes it compatible with a vegetable bed [Johnny's Selected Seeds, UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County, UC Santa Clara]
- Water by one dissenting account: low water [UC EHN 70]
- Crop coefficient: 1.0 [hand-entered, small-vegetable class after FAO-56 Table 12, cited not copied]

## Raised beds and containers
- Minimum soil depth inches: 6 [UC Tulare and Kings Master Gardener Program]
- Raised bed: fits easily [hand-entered]

## Soil and rotation
- Soil preference: light soil of average fertility [Johnny's Selected Seeds]

## Winter behaviour
- Perennial or annual depends entirely on frost: every source calling it an annual ties that to freezing, which does not happen at this site [UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County, UC Santa Clara, UC Master Gardeners of Orange County]
- Hardiness zones: 9 to 11, the only vendor range among the candidate herbs that includes zone 10b [Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Perennial or annual across UC sources: two call it a perennial outright and a third an annual, with the freeze mechanism explaining the split [UC EHN 70 and UC Santa Clara perennial, UC Tulare and Kings annual]
- Chilling requirement: none stated by any source reached
- Invasive listing: absent from the Cal-IPC inventory [Cal-IPC Inventory]

## Harvest
- Repeat harvest: yes, cut back close to the ground as it begins to bloom to force a second flush, and that second flush is the better crop [Illinois Extension]
- Cut height for a second harvest: 6 to 8 in from the ground, before first flowering [UC EHN 70]
- Harvest frequency: cut often to keep leaves tender and stop flowers forming [UC Santa Clara]
- Years of useful life: not stated by any source reached
- A hardier relative exists: Italian marjoram, Origanum x majoricum, a cross with oregano that is more winter-hardy than sweet marjoram [UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County]

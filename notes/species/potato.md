# Potato

- Botanical name: Solanum tuberosum [hand-entered]
- Family: Solanaceae [hand-entered]
- Verdict for this garden: not an October crop here, and the case against it is unusually well evidenced [hand-entered, see the timing section]

## Sowing
- Local planting months: January to March optimal, April to June acceptable, and blank July through December [UC Master Gardeners of Orange County Seed Planting Chart, confirmed against their second document under a different palette]
- Local planting months by county program: coastal February to March, plus mid August to September [UC Master Gardener Program of San Diego County]
- Planting soil temperature F: above 45 and below 70 [Potato Association of America, Commercial Potato Production in North America]
- Planting depth and spacing: not recorded here, since the crop is out of season for this site
- Propagation: from seed potatoes rather than true seed [hand-entered]

## Why October fails here, which is a timing and supply problem rather than a climate one
- The pattern across sources: every month-specific California and warm-winter source closes its fall window between 1 August and 30 September [UC Master Gardeners of Orange County, UC San Diego, LSU AgCenter, UGA, Alabama Extension, University of Arizona, Oklahoma State]
- The nearest state to say it outright: fall plantings are not recommended at all, because of increased pest and disease risk [Clemson HGIC]
- A zone-matched refusal: Texas gives 1 October for its zone 9A region and marks its warmest region, zone 9B, not recommended, and this site is warmer still at 10b [Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-056, with the last step being inference]
- Why rules from other states do not transfer: their fall dates are anchored to a first-freeze date, which is undefined at a site with no freeze [Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-077 gives 14 to 16 weeks before first freeze]
- The only October endorsements: two land-grant sources, both subtropical [UF/IFAS for South Florida, University of Hawaii CTAHR for low elevations]
- Seed supply is the binding constraint: good seed potatoes are impossible to find for fall planting [Mississippi State Extension]
- The extension workaround is to buy in spring and hold: keep spring-bought seed in a cool humid spot, and for no more than a year [Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-068]
- Dormancy has to be broken first: seed for a fall or winter planting must be aged 6 to 16 weeks depending on cultivar, stored near 55 F, then pre-sprouted for 2 to 3 weeks [Potato Association of America, Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-068]

## Temperature for tuber formation
- Ideal night temperature F: about 61, with best yields at daily means of 64 to 68 [FAO and the International Potato Center]
- Tuber formation optimum F: 50 to 60 [Oregon State Extension]
- Good yields between F: 60 and 70, and cool night temperatures matter more than cool day temperatures [University of Hawaii CTAHR]
- Where this site sits: winter mean lows near 49 F fall at or below the bottom edge of every stated optimum, so the winter is cooler than ideal rather than too warm [hand-entered, comparing the profile normals against the four sources above]
- Long nights help: tuberisation is promoted by less than 12 hours of daylight together with low temperatures, and the solstice day length here is under 10 hours [IntechOpen, Sanchez-Correa et al.]
- Upper limits and none is a winter risk here: 84 F night air, soil above 75 F reducing and above 85 F stopping tuberisation [Oregon State Extension, UC Santa Cruz]

## Raised beds and containers
- Container size: at least 2 ft deep holding about 30 gallons, which the 12 in beds and pots here do not meet [NC State Extension, and a University of Maryland page giving 30 gallons that appears to have been superseded]
- Minimum container by another account: 5 gallon or larger, which conflicts sharply with the figure above [LSU AgCenter]

## Yield, where the sources disagree fourfold
- Yield per square foot: 0.33 to 0.6 lb [NC State Extension]
- Yield per square foot by another account: 2 to 5 lb [UC Santa Cruz]
- Scale of that disagreement: four to six times on the same unit, so both are recorded rather than averaged
- Yield per hundred feet of row: 75 to 150 lb across sources [Utah State, NMSU, Texas A&M, UF/IFAS]

## Harvest
- Tuber initiation does not depend on flowering: plants will form tubers without ever flowering, and initiation runs 5 to 7 weeks after planting at 6 to 8 in of height [Potato Association of America]
- The common bloom cue is contradicted: one source ties tuber development to the start of bloom [LSU AgCenter against the Potato Association of America]
- Skin set test: the skin does not scrape off when rubbed with a thumb [Texas A&M AgriLife, LSU AgCenter]
- Vine killing: vines need killing 2 to 3 weeks before harvest, and cool cloudy weather retards vine death, which matters in a coastal winter [Potato Association of America]
- Wet-soil hazard: tubers deteriorate quickly in saturated soil, so lift early if prolonged rain is expected near the 90 day mark [LSU AgCenter]
- Curing: 55 to 60 F at high humidity for 2 to 3 weeks, then store dark at 40 to 45 F [Utah State Extension]

# Garlic

- Botanical name: Allium sativum [hand-entered]
- Family: Alliaceae [hand-entered, following the green onion entry]
- Planted from cloves and not from seed: each clove becomes one plant, and true seed is not used in ordinary cultivation [hand-entered, consistent across every source reached]

## Sowing
- Planting depth in a mild coastal winter: half an inch of soil over the clove, because depth scales with winter cold and this climate is the warm end [UC Master Gardeners, coastal guidance]
- Planting depth range: half an inch to 2 in, deeper only where winters are cold [UC Master Gardeners]
- Planting depth disagreement: figures run from just below the surface to 4 in across all sources, and the deep end is written for cold-winter regions rather than for this one
- Clove spacing inches: 4 to 6, and the wider end is what one UC county source ties to the largest bulbs [UCCE Los Angeles]
- Row spacing inches: 12, or two rows per bed on an 18 in bed top [UC ANR Table 14.2 footnote h]
- Local planting months: October to December for the South Coast, the region running from San Luis Obispo County south [UC IPM, UC ANR California Master Gardener Handbook Table 14.2]
- Local planting and harvest pairing: planted in October into a raised bed, harvested late June to July [UCCE Los Angeles]
- Local Orange County guidance: none, and garlic is absent from their Seed Planting Chart entirely [verified against the chart's full crop list and their handout library]

## Growing
- Days to harvest: 5 to 8 months, with the full spread across sources running 160 to 300 days depending on planting date and prior clove storage [Mann and Minges 1958, Hilgardia 27:385, and vendor figures]
- Bed occupancy: 8 to 10 months, so it holds the ground for most of a year [vendor guidance, consistent with the UC planting and harvest months]
- Sun: full sun all day, or at least 8 hours of direct sun [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County, UC Master Gardeners of Santa Clara County]
- Sun for softneck specifically: a position in full sun through the winter [vendor guidance]
- Tolerance of part shade: not stated by any source reached, and no source offers a reduced-light allowance
- Target soil pH: 6.0 to 6.5, with the wider tolerance running about 5.5 to 6.8 [multiple extension sources in agreement]
- Nutrient demand: moderate, preferring loose well-drained soil high in organic matter [extension consensus]
- Water: keep the top 6 in moist while plants are actively growing, and keep the soil moist through to the June harvest [UCCE Los Angeles, extension consensus]
- Crop coefficient: 1.0 [hand-entered, small-vegetable class after FAO-56 Table 12, cited not copied]

## Raised beds and containers
- Soil preparation depth inches: at least 8, preferably 12 [extension consensus]
- Raised bed height inches: 10 to 12 where soil is heavy [extension guidance]
- Root depth: shallow, with most roots in the upper 2 ft, though roots can reach much deeper in loose soil [extension sources]
- Container depth inches: one vendor asks for 20, which is the outlier against the 8 to 12 in preparation figure every extension source gives [San Diego Seed Company against extension consensus]

## Soil and rotation
- Do not follow: Alliaceae [hand-entered, following the green onion entry]

## Bulbing and cold requirement
- The threshold that matters for a frost-free site: plants never exposed to temperatures below 68 F may fail to form bulbs at all, even under long days [Mann and Minges 1958, Hilgardia 27:385, California Agricultural Experiment Station]
- Why that threshold is reachable here: a winter whose mean high is about 63 F sits below 68 F, so the requirement is met by the climate rather than needing to be supplied [hand-entered, comparing the profile's normals against Mann and Minges]
- The optimum is colder than the threshold: 1 to 2 months at 32 to 41 F gives the fastest bulbing, and about 4 weeks is near the minimum that alters bulbing at all [Mann and Minges 1958]
- Storage temperature decides the outcome in a controlled test: stock stored at 68 F formed no bulbs in a warm greenhouse, while stock stored at 41 F bulbed readily [Mann and Minges 1958]
- A stricter county figure this climate cannot meet: 4 to 6 weeks below 40 to 45 F, which a site with no day at or below 36 F never reaches [UC county Master Gardener program]
- The two are reconciled by variety and not by climate: softneck types are the answer, and every source that distinguishes the groups says so [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego, Sonoma and Contra Costa counties]
- Softneck needs less chilling: it does not need the extended chill hours that hardneck does, and it tolerates warmer temperatures [UC Master Gardeners of Contra Costa County, UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County]
- Softneck in California: the main commercial type grown in the state [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Groups to choose from for a warm region: Artichoke, Silverskin and Creole, with California Early and Creole named for warm California and California Late explicitly ruled out for the southern desert [UC production bulletin]
- Chill hours: no source anywhere states a chill-hour figure for garlic, nor addresses zone 10b or Sunset zone 24 by name
- Pre-chilling cloves in a refrigerator: suggested as insurance by a local vendor and by one out-of-state extension service, but UC advises against it, and neither source recommending it states a temperature [conflicting guidance, recorded unresolved]

## Failure modes
- Commonest cause of failure in California: waterlogging rather than insufficient chilling [GardenZeus]
- Single rounds instead of a divided bulb: documented in coastal Southern California and blamed on late planting rather than on a warm winter [UCCE Los Angeles]
- Likely poor outcome on a light-limited site: small bulbs rather than total failure, because bulb size follows the foliage built before bulbing begins [hand-entered, following the consensus that foliage precedes bulb size]

## Harvest
- Repeat harvest: no, a single lift per clove planted
- Scapes: hardneck types produce a flower stalk worth removing, which softneck types largely do not [hand-entered, consistent across sources]

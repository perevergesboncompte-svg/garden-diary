# Onion, bulb

- Botanical name: Allium cepa [hand-entered]
- Family: Alliaceae [hand-entered, following the green onion entry]
- This file is bulb onion: green onion is a separate crop with its own file, and several sources treat the two differently on sun and container depth [hand-entered]

## Day length, which decides the variety before anything else
- Bulbing trigger: day length, so the variety class has to match the site before a planting date means anything [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Short day is the class for this garden: short day suits roughly 25 to 35 degrees north, intermediate 32 to 42, long day 37 to 47, and this site sits at 33.6 [Dixondale Farms, Johnny's Selected Seeds]
- Long day types: do not bulb well this far south, and are often what is sold as sets [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Measured day length here: 11.40 h on 15 October, 9.97 h at the solstice, 12.18 h on 20 March, 14.44 h at midsummer, so the long day trigger is never reached [computed from the site's own coordinates]
- Bulbing start date here: about 20 March, when day length crosses 12 h [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Bulbing trigger hours: sources give 10 to 12, about 12, and 12 to 14, and the answer is unchanged because every variant is satisfied at some point [UC Master Gardeners of Orange County, UC Davis VRIC, UC IPM]
- Named short day cultivars for this area: Grano, Granex sold as Yellow Granex Hybrid, Crystal Wax or White Bermuda, and California Early Red [UC Davis VRIC, UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Bolt resistant cultivar: Texas Grano 502 [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Avoid: Walla Walla Sweet, which does not bulb well here, and treat day neutral labels with suspicion [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County, UC Master Gardeners of Orange County]
- A conflicting cultivar note worth heeding: Texas Grano 1015Y is rated poor for bolting resistance and very susceptible when direct seeded [New Mexico State University]

## Sowing
- Local sowing months once split by variety class: October from seed for short day types, November to December for short day transplants, February to March for intermediate day types [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Why the UC sources appear to conflict: each gives one window per region without splitting by variety class, so the October, the November to December and the February to March windows are describing different variety and propagation combinations rather than disagreeing [hand-entered, following the San Diego source that does split them]
- Why UC appears to contradict itself: it is an edition revision rather than a conflict, the 2002 first edition giving the South Coast February to March and the 2015 second edition revising that same cell to February to October [UC ANR Master Gardener Handbook Table 14.2 against Table 13.2]
- Current UC position: includes October, being the newer edition and the one the live page reproduces [UC ANR Master Gardener Handbook Table 13.2]
- Sowing months on the third-party chart: January, February, November and December, with October blank, which is now the outlier against three UC sources [SoCal planting chart, third-party, republished by UC Master Gardeners]
- Corroborating October: south of Bakersfield, seeded or transplanted in October for a June harvest [UC Davis VRIC]
- Sets: ruled out by two UC sources, because they tend to bolt rather than make a large bulb [UC Davis VRIC, UC IPM]
- Planting depth: set so the bulb is barely covered, because deeper planting gives shoots rather than bulbs [UC Master Gardeners of Orange County]
- Plant spacing inches: 3 in the printed table, against 4 to 5 for transplants and 4 to 6 as a final spacing elsewhere [UC ANR Table 14.2, UC IPM, UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Spacing decides bulb size: thin to 2 in for green onions, 4 in for moderate bulbs, 8 in for large bulbs [University of Maryland Extension]
- Row spacing inches: 18, and the crop carries UC's bed footnote, so two rows fit an 18 in bed top [UC ANR Table 14.2]
- Why October beats February here: bulbing starts around 20 March regardless, so an October sowing builds leaves for about five months first where a February sowing gets about six weeks, and bulb size follows leaf number, with about 13 leaves the target [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]

## Growing
- Days to harvest: 90 to 130 from seed depending on source, or about 6 months [UC Davis VRIC]
- Local harvest month: June or July from an October sowing [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Sun: sources spread widely, from full sun all day, through at least 6 h, down to at least 4 to 6 h [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County, University of Maryland Extension, UC IPM]
- Partial shade: granted by one land-grant container table, which assigns bulb onion partial shade while assigning green onion full sun, though with no hours figure behind it [University of Kentucky ID-128 Table 20.10]
- Partial shade withheld elsewhere: one source grants it to green onion and garlic while withholding it from bulb onion and shallot on the same page [Virginia Cooperative Extension 426-411]
- A notable absence: no source reached states that bulb onions fail to bulb, or make small bulbs, because of shade, with every such failure attributed instead to day length, planting date, spacing, water or fertility [hand-entered, after targeted searching]
- The nearest thing to a shade warning: overcast skies and cool temperatures during the season will delay bulb formation [University of Minnesota Extension]
- Yield per square foot: 0.5 to 0.8 lb, which is the joint highest figure in the table it comes from [NC State Extension]
- Yield per ten feet of row: 20 to 25 lb against 7 to 10 lb and 10 lb elsewhere, a disagreement left unresolved [University of Maryland Extension, Virginia Tech, UF and University of Kentucky]
- Crop coefficient: 1.0 [hand-entered, small-vegetable class after FAO-56 Table 12, cited not copied]

## Raised beds and containers
- Root depth: shallow, and UC lists onion among its shallow-rooted crops [UC ANR Soil and Water Management for the Vegetable Garden]
- Keep moist to a depth of inches: 6, the crop being shallow rooted [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- Container depth inches: 6 [University of Kentucky ID-128]
- Container authorities that omit bulb onion entirely: three of them, listing only scallions or green onions [NC State Extension chapter 18, University of Maryland Extension, Colorado State GardenNotes]
- Raised bed: no source found requires more than 10 in, so a 12 in bed exceeds every published figure [hand-entered]

## Bolting
- Bolting is driven by temperature once the plant is big enough: plants with a leaf base over about three eighths of an inch in diameter, exposed to 45 to 50 F [UC ANR Publication 7242]
- Where this site sits: December, January and February mean lows of 49.3, 48.8 and 48.9 F fall inside that band [hand-entered, comparing the profile normals against Publication 7242]
- A conflicting UC threshold: bolting occurs below 45 F for a sufficient length of time, which this site's winter mean lows stay above [UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County]
- This conflict does not resolve: both figures are UC, and one puts the winter inside the risk band while the other puts it outside [hand-entered]
- Daytime means are also in the vernalising range by one table: receptive at 4 to 6 leaves, optimum 48 to 55 F, maximum 64 F, over 4 to 12 weeks, with reversal only above 68 F, against local winter mean highs of 62.9 to 63.8 F [MSU Extension]
- A documented fall-planting failure in a mild California climate: fall-started onions flowering the following spring and then unable to make a good bulb, with February planting advised instead [UC Master Gardeners of Contra Costa County]
- The mitigation that follows from the threshold being about size: sow seed in October rather than setting out large transplants, so the plant stays under the three eighths of an inch leaf base through the coldest weeks [hand-entered, following Publication 7242]

# Garden Journal

## 17.10.2026

### Something is stripping the peas and spinach, and photographs cannot say what
- Action: observed
- Plants: peas, spinach, lettuce
- Details: Pea leaflets skeletonised with the veins left standing, several stripped to bare
  petioles, and one spinach plant of six reduced to bare midribs while its five neighbours
  stay untouched.
- Learnings: Slugs, earwigs and young caterpillars all produce this and no photographic
  feature separates them. Skeletonising in particular points away from caterpillars rather
  than towards them: slug damage is described as removal of plant tissue between the veins
  and on the leaf edges, which covers both damage types here in one sentence, while a
  damage key puts skeletonising with slugs and beetles and puts caterpillars under entire
  leaves consumed [Pacific Northwest Insect Management Handbook, NC State Extension].
  Earwig damage on seedlings is described as missing all or parts of their leaves and stem,
  which UC itself notes may resemble caterpillar feeding.
- Learnings: Webbing is the one trace no mollusc and no earwig leaves, so it beats frass as
  a caterpillar test. Slime is what separates molluscs from the other two. No source
  reachable describes any way to make dried slime visible.
- Note: An earlier entry here named a caterpillar on the strength of dark specks read as
  frass on a pea leaflet. At full resolution those marks are diffuse, sit in the leaf plane
  rather than on it and could be necrotic tissue or a collapsed mine, so the frass is
  withdrawn and the identification with it. The leafminer identification from the previous
  week stands, and is a separate minor problem whose adult is the small fly the gardener
  saw.
- Note: Measured cover on the spinach frame fell from 6.405 to 4.815 percent while the
  plants visibly grew, which only happens if foliage was removed. The pea bed is otherwise
  dense and thriving, and the stakes are now in.

### Carrots approaching the thinning threshold
- Action: observed
- Plants: carrots
- Details: Feathery true leaves now, rather than the single threads of a week ago. Median
  leaf spread scaled against the known 21 in bed width was 1.02 in on 10 October.
- Learnings: An overhead photograph measures leaf spread and cannot measure height, which
  is what the packet's one-inch thinning trigger is stated in. The two are not
  interchangeable and the gardener needs a ruler for that call.

  merging into one blob, not of plants dying.

## 10.10.2026

### Four herbs sown into their own pots
- Action: sowed
- Plants: basil, oregano, parsley, cilantro
- Details: Basil, True Greek oregano, parsley and cilantro each into an individual pot.
- Note: Oregano went in as seed rather than as a bought plant. Two things decide whether it
  works. The seed has an absolute light requirement, so it must sit pressed onto the surface
  and not be covered, and seed-grown oregano is variable in flavour where a nursery plant is
  not. If nothing germinates in 7 to 14 days, burial is the first thing to suspect.

### What the six photos show
- Action: observed
- Plants: carrots, spinach, peas, cilantro, dill, lettuce
- Details: Carrots up in all 4 rows at thread stage, clumped rather than evenly spaced, not
  yet an inch tall. Cilantro and dill in the shared pot both healthy and deep green, with
  cardboard dividers between the sections. No parsley showing in its section of that pot.
  Lettuce at cotyledon stage. Peas sprawling with tendrils already reaching for something
  to climb.
- Learnings: The growing medium reads as a bark-heavy mulch in every photo rather than a
  garden or potting mix, which is worth knowing but is not currently causing a symptom.
  Measuring leaf colour in the photos puts spinach at hue 79.6 degrees against dill at 78 to
  80, with near-identical RGB on the lit patches, so the two are the same green. An earlier
  reading of the spinach as pale and nitrogen-short came from comparing a shaded spinach leaf
  against a lit dill leaf, lightness in that one photo running from 36 to 57 percent. Colour
  judgements from a photo need a lit-to-lit comparison or a measurement, not an eyeball
  across two frames.
- Note: Pea support is now overdue, the tendrils having nothing to hold.

### Sun exposure measured, setup recorded
- Action: observed
- Plants: all
- Details: Reported window is shade until about 13:30, then direct sun to roughly one
  hour before sunset. Measured against hourly Open-Meteo sunshine data: 4.0 to 4.9 h
  in mid-September against 9.0 h open sky, and 2.5 h over December 2025 on the same
  clock window. Part sun now, part shade in winter.
- Note: Exposure is afternoon-only, which is inverted from what cool-season greens
  want. Heat and bolting are the standing risk, not light shortage. December is the
  binding limitation because winter is the productive season in this climate.

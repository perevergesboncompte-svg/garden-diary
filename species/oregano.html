Oregano

Everything recorded

Botanical name
Origanum vulgare, and Greek oregano is subsp. hirtum Missouri Botanical Garden
Family
Lamiaceae hand-entered
Buy by subspecies and not by common name
plain Origanum vulgare lacks the depth of oregano flavour wanted in cooking and is sold elsewhere as a dried-flower crop called Wild Marjoram, so the culinary plant is subsp. hirtum UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County, Johnny's Selected Seeds

Sowing

Sowing depth mm
0, surface sown, because the seed has an absolute light requirement Thanos, Kadis and Skarou 1995, Seed Science Research 5:161
Light needed to germinate
yes, absolutely, and germination is promoted even by green safelight or far-red light, measured on subsp. hirtum specifically Thanos, Kadis and Skarou 1995
Germination percentage
74 to 77 under good conditions Mijani et al. 2013, Notulae Scientia Biologicae
Germination optimum temperature
unresolved between two peer-reviewed papers, 15 to 20 C against about 30 C Thanos 1995, Mijani 2013
Germination base temperature C
5.46, with a maximum of 42.91 Mijani et al. 2013
Germination difficulty rating
no special requirements, but germination may be slow and erratic Territorial Seed germination code 4
Cold stratification
not required, and it is not assigned the vendor code that denotes a stratification need Territorial Seed
Seed comes variable in flavour
three UC county sources and Illinois all warn that seed-grown oregano is variable and may lack flavour, and one UC-hosted document lists oregano among herbs that should be bought as plants rather than sown UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County, Illinois Extension, NC State Extension HIL-8110 hosted on UC ANR
Local sowing months
UC Marin marks oregano for planting starts outdoors in April, May and September, and says spring, summer or fall. No UC source recommends a September or October sowing from seed rather than from starts UC Marin Master Gardeners

Growing

Mature size of Greek oregano
6 to 9 in tall with a 12 to 18 in spread, and described as a more compact plant at 6 to 8 in Missouri Botanical Garden
Mature size of the straight species
1 to 3 ft, so much larger than the culinary subspecies Missouri Botanical Garden
Sun
full sun or partial shade, where the partial shade band is 2 to 6 h of direct sun a day NC State Extension
Sun compared with marjoram
NC State gives oregano the partial shade tag and gives sweet marjoram only the full sun tag, which makes oregano the better of the two for a part-shade bed NC State Extension
Water for Greek oregano
medium, against dry to medium for the straight species, so the culinary subspecies is the one that tolerates a vegetable bed's irrigation Missouri Botanical Garden
Water by UC account
moderate for culinary oregano, low for the non-culinary ones UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County
Rich soil costs flavour
grown in rich soil it may lose its pungent scent, which a compost-heavy vegetable bed will do Johnny's Selected Seeds

Harvest

Repeat harvest
yes, an established planting can be cut back two to six times in a growing season, which is more cuts than sweet marjoram's two flushes Johnny's Selected Seeds
Replacement interval years
4, agreed by two independent sources Johnny's Selected Seeds, UC Marin Master Gardeners
Harvest timing
cut before flowering, because leaf production declines once it flowers Illinois Extension
Cut fraction
remove about a third of the growth at any one time, the general rule for perennial herbs Illinois Extension

Comparison note

Against rosemary from seed
oregano germinates at 74 to 77 percent where untreated rosemary seed measured 9.7 percent, which is why oregano is a seed crop and rosemary is not Mijani et al. 2013, Sharma, Fagan and Schaefer 2019, Biological Agriculture and Horticulture
Against thyme at sowing
thyme's germination is light indifferent so it can be covered, where oregano must not be Thanos, Kadis and Skarou 1995

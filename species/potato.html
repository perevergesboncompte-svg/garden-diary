Why October fails here, which is a timing and supply problem rather than a climate one

The pattern across sources
every month-specific California and warm-winter source closes its fall window between 1 August and 30 September UC Master Gardeners of Orange County, UC San Diego, LSU AgCenter, UGA, Alabama Extension, University of Arizona, Oklahoma State
The nearest state to say it outright
fall plantings are not recommended at all, because of increased pest and disease risk Clemson HGIC
A zone-matched refusal
Texas gives 1 October for its zone 9A region and marks its warmest region, zone 9B, not recommended, and this site is warmer still at 10b Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-056, with the last step being inference
Why rules from other states do not transfer
their fall dates are anchored to a first-freeze date, which is undefined at a site with no freeze Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-077 gives 14 to 16 weeks before first freeze
The only October endorsements
two land-grant sources, both subtropical UF/IFAS for South Florida, University of Hawaii CTAHR for its lowland zone
Seed supply is the binding constraint
good seed potatoes are impossible to find for fall planting Mississippi State Extension
The extension workaround is to buy in spring and hold
keep spring-bought seed in a cool humid spot, and for no more than a year Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-068
Dormancy has to be broken first
seed for a fall or winter planting must be aged 6 to 16 weeks depending on cultivar, stored near 55 F, then pre-sprouted for 2 to 3 weeks Potato Association of America, Texas A&M AgriLife EHT-068

Temperature for tuber formation

Ideal night temperature F
about 61, with best yields at daily means of 64 to 68 FAO and the International Potato Center
Tuber formation optimum F
50 to 60 Oregon State Extension
Good yields between F
60 and 70, and cool night temperatures matter more than cool day temperatures University of Hawaii CTAHR
Where this site sits
winter mean lows near 49 F fall at or below the bottom edge of every stated optimum, so the winter is cooler than ideal rather than too warm hand-entered, comparing the profile normals against the four sources above
Long nights help
tuberisation is promoted by less than 12 hours of daylight together with low temperatures, and the solstice day length here is under 10 hours IntechOpen, Sanchez-Correa et al.
Upper limits and none is a winter risk here
84 F night air, soil above 75 F reducing and above 85 F stopping tuberisation Oregon State Extension, UC Santa Cruz

Harvest

Tuber initiation does not depend on flowering
plants will form tubers without ever flowering, and initiation runs 5 to 7 weeks after planting at 6 to 8 in of height Potato Association of America
The common bloom cue is contradicted
one source ties tuber development to the start of bloom LSU AgCenter against the Potato Association of America
Skin set test
the skin does not scrape off when rubbed with a thumb Texas A&M AgriLife, LSU AgCenter
Vine killing
vines need killing 2 to 3 weeks before harvest, and cool cloudy weather retards vine death, which matters in a coastal winter Potato Association of America
Wet-soil hazard
tubers deteriorate quickly in saturated soil, so lift early if prolonged rain is expected near the 90 day mark LSU AgCenter
Curing
55 to 60 F at high humidity for 2 to 3 weeks, then store dark at 40 to 45 F Utah State Extension

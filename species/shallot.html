Day length, which decides whether a variety works at all

Bulbing is photoperiod controlled
onions and their relatives are classed long or short day by the hours of daylight needed to form a bulb Clemson HGIC
Short day types
bulb at about 12 hours of daylight and suit gardens in the south Clemson HGIC
Long day types
cannot be grown successfully in the South Clemson HGIC
Consequence this far south
a long day variety may grow leaves perfectly and never form a usable bulb, so its day length class matters more than flavour or price hand-entered, following Clemson

Bolting

A flowered plant is a lost crop
once an onion, shallot or leek flowers it will not form a marketable bulb or stalk MSU Extension
Fall planting lowers the risk
fall-transplanted shallots are less likely to bolt than spring-sown alliums, because of a larger juvenile size requirement and longer vernalising time MSU Extension

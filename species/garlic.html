Bulbing and cold requirement

The threshold that matters for a frost-free site
plants never exposed to temperatures below 68 F may fail to form bulbs at all, even under long days Mann and Minges 1958, Hilgardia 27:385, California Agricultural Experiment Station
Why that threshold is reachable here
a winter whose mean high is about 63 F sits below 68 F, so the requirement is met by the climate rather than needing to be supplied hand-entered, comparing the profile's normals against Mann and Minges
The optimum is colder than the threshold
1 to 2 months at 32 to 41 F gives the fastest bulbing, and about 4 weeks is near the minimum that alters bulbing at all Mann and Minges 1958
Storage temperature decides the outcome in a controlled test
stock stored at 68 F formed no bulbs in a warm greenhouse, while stock stored at 41 F bulbed readily Mann and Minges 1958
A stricter county figure this climate cannot meet
4 to 6 weeks below 40 to 45 F, which a site with no day at or below 36 F never reaches UC county Master Gardener program
The two are reconciled by variety and not by climate
softneck types are the answer, and every source that distinguishes the groups says so UC Master Gardeners of San Diego, Sonoma and Contra Costa counties
Softneck needs less chilling
it does not need the extended chill hours that hardneck does, and it tolerates warmer temperatures UC Master Gardeners of Contra Costa County, UC Master Gardeners of Sonoma County
Softneck in California
the main commercial type grown in the state UC Master Gardeners of San Diego County
